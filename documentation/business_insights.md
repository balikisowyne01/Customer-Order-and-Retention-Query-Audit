\# Business Insights and Recommendations





**## 1. Project Overview**



This project focuses on analysing customer and order data using PostgreSQL to understand customer purchasing behaviour, sales performance, and customer retention.



The analysis involves examining customer records, order patterns, product purchases, revenue, and repeat purchasing behaviour. SQL queries are used to extract, organise, and analyse the data to identify meaningful business insights.



The overall goal is to understand how customers interact with the business and provide data-driven recommendations that can support better business decisions.





**## 2. Key Findings**



The analysis of the customer and order dataset revealed the following:



1\. \*\*Customer Base:\*\* The business has a total of 10 registered customers.



2\. \*\*Order Volume:\*\* A total of 15 orders were recorded in the database.



3\. \*\*Repeat Customers:\*\* Some customers placed multiple orders, indicating repeat purchasing behaviour.



4\. \*\*Customer Revenue:\*\* Daniel generated the highest customer revenue, while Mary generated the lowest based on the calculated order values.



5\. \*\*Average Order Value:\*\* The average order value was approximately 223,466.67.



6\. \*\*Customer Retention:\*\* The retention rate between January and February was 33.33%, meaning that 1 out of 3 January customers made another purchase in February.



7\. \*\*Monthly Customer Activity:\*\* The analysis examined the number of unique customers placing orders each month to understand purchasing activity over time.





**## 3. Business Insights**



Based on the analysis conducted, the following business insights were identified:



1\. \*\*Customer Purchasing Behaviour:\*\* The presence of repeat customers shows that some customers are willing to purchase from the business more than once.



2\. \*\*Customer Revenue Contribution:\*\* Daniel generated the highest customer revenue, while Mary generated the lowest. This indicates differences in the purchasing value contributed by individual customers.



3\. \*\*Customer Retention:\*\* The January–February retention rate of 33.33% suggests that only a small proportion of January's active customers returned to purchase in February.



4\. \*\*Order Value:\*\* The average order value of 223,466.67 provides a baseline for monitoring the value of customer transactions.



5\. \*\*Monthly Customer Activity:\*\* Tracking unique customers across different months helps the business understand changes in customer activity and identify returning purchasing patterns.



Overall, the analysis highlights the importance of monitoring customer purchasing behaviour, order values, and retention patterns to understand business performance.





**## 4. Business Recommendations**



Based on the findings and insights from the analysis, the following recommendations are proposed:



1\. \*\*Improve Customer Retention:\*\* The business should develop customer engagement strategies, such as follow-up messages, loyalty rewards, and personalised offers, to encourage previous customers to make repeat purchases.



2\. \*\*Monitor Customer Purchasing Behaviour:\*\* The business should regularly analyse customer ordering patterns to identify frequent buyers and understand changes in purchasing behaviour.



3\. \*\*Improve Customer Engagement:\*\* Customers with low purchasing activity should be studied to understand possible reasons for their reduced engagement.



4\. \*\*Monitor Average Order Value:\*\* The business should track its average order value regularly and explore suitable product combinations or promotions that may encourage customers to purchase additional items.



5\. \*\*Recognise High-Value Customers:\*\* The business should identify and appreciate customers who contribute significantly to its sales while maintaining good relationships with all customers.



6\. \*\*Monitor Order Status:\*\* The business should regularly review pending, cancelled, returned, and delivered orders to identify operational issues and improve order fulfilment.



These recommendations can help the business improve customer relationships, understand purchasing behaviour, and make better data-driven decisions.





**## 5. Conclusion**



This project involved analysing customer and order data using PostgreSQL to understand purchasing behaviour, order performance, and customer retention.



Through SQL queries, different analyses were performed, including customer segmentation, repeat purchase identification, revenue calculations, average order value, monthly customer activity, and customer retention rate.



The analysis provided useful insights into customer purchasing patterns and highlighted areas where the business could improve customer engagement and retention.



Overall, this project strengthened my practical knowledge of PostgreSQL, data analysis, data manipulation, and the use of SQL to solve business-related problems.



The project demonstrates how structured data can be transformed into meaningful information to support informed business decisions.
